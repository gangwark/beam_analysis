Optimizes for the span, loading and material currently set in the Beam Lab. The minimum-thickness rule is why the answer stays buildable: beam theory happily accepts paper-thin walls that would buckle in reality. Envelope is the available space: every candidate must fit inside the original sketch’s bounding box × this factor — without it, “stiffest” degenerates to “grow deeper forever,” since I ~ d³. L/360 is the classic building-code serviceability limit for floor live load under brittle finishes (≈ the deflection at which plaster cracks); L/240 is common for total load, L/180 for roofs. It is adjustable because a deflection limit is a serviceability choice, not physics.

Each sample draws random requirements (span, loads, material, objective weight), picks a compatible family + process, runs a short optimization, and records the optimized dimensions with full performance and manufacturing metrics — an inverse-design dataset: requirements in, best manufacturable section out.

Section: Green’s theorem on polygon contours. Beam: statics + exact piecewise V, M; deflection by double integration of M/EI. Free-form optimizer: simulated annealing on sketch vertices with geometric validity gates. Design Studio: parametric section families with process-aware rules — AISC-style flange/web compactness, minimum gauges, die limits, weld-length and tooling-amortized costing — plus active-constraint and sensitivity reporting.
